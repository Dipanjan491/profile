02 — Philosophy & Background

The mind, like weather, arrives — and, given room, passes.

Most distress isn't a malfunction to be fixed. It's a signal — sometimes loud, sometimes old — asking to be understood. My clinical psychology practice begins here: not with urgency, but with attention. And not with labels, but with careful measurement that illuminates rather than reduces.

As both a clinician and a psychometrician, I inhabit two worlds that are too often separated. In one, the human being in front of me is the primary data. In the other, it's the rigorous statistical models — Item Response Theory, latent profile analysis, construct validation — that help us see structure in what otherwise feels like noise.

Good measurement and good therapy share the same posture: curiosity without presumption.
